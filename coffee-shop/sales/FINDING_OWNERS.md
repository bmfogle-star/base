# Finding Coffee Shop Owners — the playbook

_You sell remote (Zoom/Loom), so this is a list-building + outreach system.
Target: 10 real conversations. Expect roughly 30 shops contacted → 10
responses → 5 demos → 2–3 founding signatures. Those are normal numbers._

## Step 1 — Build the list (1 evening, aim for 30 shops)

Work Google Maps first — it's the best database of coffee shops on earth:
- Search "coffee shop" over your city + 2–3 neighboring towns.
- **Qualify:** independent (not a chain), 4.3+ stars with 100+ reviews
  (healthy business), photos showing morning lines or a busy counter.
- **Disqualify:** franchises, shops inside grocery stores/hotels, anywhere
  whose reviews complain about the owner being absent.

For each shop, grab into a spreadsheet: name, Instagram handle, website,
phone, owner's first name if visible (About page, news articles, "meet the
owner" posts), and one specific detail you noticed (their signature drink,
their dog, their mural). That detail is your opener.

## Step 2 — Find the actual owner (5 min per shop)

- **Instagram is the #1 channel.** Indie shop accounts are almost always run
  by the owner. Check the bio, tagged photos, and "our story" highlights.
- The shop's website About page; local news ("Best coffee in ___" articles
  name owners); Yelp's "From the business" section.
- Your state's business registry (free search) lists the LLC owner's name if
  you're unsure who's in charge.
- Walking in still works when you're nearby: buy a coffee at 2–4pm and ask
  the barista, "Is the owner around much? I'd love to send them something."
  Baristas will happily tell you when the owner works.

## Step 3 — First touch (in order of what works)

1. **Instagram DM** (best reply rate for indie shops):
   > "Hey [name] — love what you've built at [shop], the [specific detail]
   > is great. I build order-ahead + rewards apps for independent shops
   > (think Starbucks app, but it's YOURS — your brand, 0% of your sales).
   > Made a 2-min video showing exactly how it works — want me to send it?"
   Asking permission to send the Loom ("want me to send it?") doubles the
   odds it gets watched vs. dumping a link cold.
2. **Email** (if no IG): same message, subject "Your own app — 2 min demo
   (no pitch deck)". Send Tue–Thu, 7–9am — owners do email before open.
3. **The counter visit** for shops in your own town: hand the barista a
   one-pager with your QR code, "could you pass this to [owner]? It's a demo
   they can try on their phone." Follow up on IG two days later.
4. **Referral engine (activate after shop #1):** "Which other owner do you
   know who'd want this?" Indie coffee is a tight community — owners all know
   each other from farmers markets and roaster relationships. One happy shop
   → two warm intros. This becomes your main channel.

Also worth joining: your city's small-business Facebook groups, the local
chamber of commerce mixer (owners attend these), and any "indie coffee
[city]" community. Local roasters are gold: one roaster supplies 20 shops
and can intro you to all of them — pitch the roaster on looking like a hero
to their accounts.

## Step 4 — Follow-up cadence (where 80% of yeses come from)

- Day 0: DM/email → Day 3: send/re-surface the Loom → Day 7: one-line nudge
  ("30 seconds: [link] — worth a look before I fill the founding five").
- **Stop after 3 touches.** Move on; recycle the list next quarter.
- Track every touch in the spreadsheet. Boring, decisive, non-optional.

## The self-demo close (your unfair advantage)

Anyone who shows interest gets:
> "Don't take my word for it — open this on your phone right now and order
> something: [demo URL]. That's what your customers would get, with your
> name on it."
The QR/link **is** the pitch. Very few salespeople can hand an owner a
working product 30 seconds into a conversation. You can.
